# The Department of Unclaimed Rooms: Scene Outline

Catalogue slot 01.01 | 18 scenes in three acts | Three locations: the building, the Register Room, the caretaker's room

## Governing rules

- **Two separate problems run together, and Ilse's error is binding them.**
  - *Problem A, what the building is.* Which rooms exist, and who sees them under which rule.
  - *Problem B, who is rehoused.* An allocation under a fixed pool of 10 places.
  - Ilse assumes that if she makes the map accurate, the allocation will become fair. It does not, because the allocation is computed from whichever classification the map carries.
- **No supernatural content.** Every room can be measured. The unease comes from paper.
- **The territory stays measurable.** Ilse's tape and scanner readings are never in doubt. What changes from scene to scene is what the measurement is allowed to count as.
- **Every correction is announced in the scene it happens and its effect is stated in the next.** The corrections ledger from the treatment (C1 restore, C2 withdraw, C3 proof-count rule) is the spine of Act Two.
- **The countdown.** Each scene opens on the register terminal's header: days until works begin, and the count of dwellings the register currently holds.

## Facts fixed by the treatment (do not drift)

- 48 listed flats, 5 rear rooms (2R to 6R), 6 people in the rear rooms (a couple in 2R, one each in 3R, 4R, 5R, 6R), so 5 households.
- Rear rooms measure 7.4 to 8.6 m2 and open on a 2.4 m light well. Standard: 9 m2, window on a street or court of at least 3 m, door onto a shared corridor.
- Phase one (north stack): 8 listed households plus rear rooms 4R, 5R, 6R, making 11 households against 10 rehousing places.
- Three listed flats are empty but counted as occupied. With those, the pool is 13 places.
- Day 0 is the day the contractor takes the north stack.

## Act One: A mismatch (Days 30 to 25)

**Scene 01. The scanner.** Exterior and stairwell. Day 30. Count: 48.
Ilse sets up a laser scanner in the lobby and works up the central stair with a tape and a tablet. Gordon Pike lets her through each floor. By the third floor her tablet shows the building's volume exceeding the sum of its drawn rooms. She assumes a scanning error and recalibrates.
*Live:* O1. *Visual:* the first building section, clean, with the rear strip hatched.

**Scene 02. The service door.** Second floor corridor. Day 30.
Behind a narrow door on the second-floor service landing she finds a room with a bed, a kettle, a radio and a man writing a letter. His partner is at work. She apologizes and checks the plan on her tablet. The strip is blank. The man (Room 2R) produces his electoral card without being asked.
*Visual:* the door drawn flush with the corridor wall, barely there.

**Scene 03. Five rooms.** Floors 3 to 6, rear strip. Day 29.
She finds four more rooms. In each, the resident shows her papers before she asks. Teodor Kask (5R) opens a folder of six records. Ottilie Brandt (3R) brings a shoebox, mostly in her late husband's name. Ilse measures each room: all between 7.4 and 8.6 m2, with windows onto a narrow well.
*Live:* O3. *Visual:* each resident's documents laid out as small tidy plates.

**Scene 04. The ledger.** Caretaker's room. Day 28.
Ilse asks Gordon about the rooms. He opens a ledger, then a drawer of handwritten slips. "It is on my ledger." He says nothing more, and she does not press, because she does not yet know what the ledger is.
*Live:* O6. *Visual:* the ledger as a flat observed object; the key board behind him with five unmarked hooks.

**Scene 05. The plan is the plan.** Register Room. Day 27.
Ilse reports to Desmond Okafor. He pulls the 1974 plan and shows her the rear strip, blank, outside the scope of the works. He asks only whether the rooms are real. She says they are. He takes down the manual, opens the dwelling standard, and reads the thresholds aloud. He also mentions the rehousing plan: 10 places, computed from the register. He suspects the proofs are overstated (R2) but does not insist.
*Live:* O2, O7, R2. *Visual:* the register terminal, the manual open beside it, the thirty-day header.

**Scene 06. The wrong plan.** Heritage archive. Day 25.
Ilse crosses to the heritage archive, a different office, and asks for the 1931 drawings. The five rooms appear as staff rooms. She concludes that the 1974 plan is a defective record and that restoring the rooms will repair it (R1). She says so to the archivist, who asks only what category she plans to restore them as. She does not hear the question.
*Live:* O4, R1. *Visual:* the 1931 drawing placed over the 1974 plan; the match is almost perfect.

## Act Two: Corrections select (Days 23 to 9)

**Scene 07. Restored.** Register Room, night. Day 22. Count: 48, then 53.
Ilse enters the five rooms as habitable rooms, correction C1. The count on the header turns to 53. She goes home.
*Visual:* the section drawn complete for the first time, an orderly panel. It is the last panel that looks finished.

**Scene 08. The morning after.** Register Room. Day 21.
The validator has run against the Housing Standard overnight. All five rooms fail on area and light well, and five closing notices have been generated with 28-day periods. The rehousing plan now counts 11 households for 10 places. The manual ranks by documented length of tenancy. The newest household in the stack is the couple in 6C, tenants since 2024.
*Live:* C1's effects. *Visual:* the ranking table, with a thin line drawn under place ten.

**Scene 09. Notice.** Rear landing, 5R. Day 20.
Teodor opens his notice. His courier firm checks addresses against closing notices and suspends him that afternoon, for nine days. Ottilie reads hers on her own landing and folds it small.
*Visual:* notices as stamped documents, five identical forms.

**Scene 10. 6C.** Sixth floor. Day 19.
Ilse visits the couple in 6C, who have an infant and no idea there is a problem. The letter they have received says phase-one decant places are unavailable to them. She cannot explain the ranking without describing her own correction.
*Visual:* a warm domestic panel, the only one in the book so far.
