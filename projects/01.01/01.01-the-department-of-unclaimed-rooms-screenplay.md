# THE DEPARTMENT OF UNCLAIMED ROOMS

Screenplay, catalogue slot 01.01
Draft 1, built on the scene outline

---

## Conventions used on the page

- **TERMINAL HEADER.** Each scene opens on the register terminal's header: days until works begin, and the register's count of dwellings. It is an observed object, drawn hard-edged.
- **SECTIONS.** Building sections are drawn from one fixed viewpoint at one fixed scale. Each register has its own section. Slugs name which one is on the page.
- **No supernatural content.** Everything in the building can be measured. The strangeness is in the paper.

# ACT ONE: A MISMATCH

---

## 01. THE SCANNER

TERMINAL HEADER: DAY 30 / DWELLINGS 48

EXT. CALLOWAY HOUSE - MORNING

A six-storey brick building, once a hotel, with a lettered fascia still faintly legible above the lobby doors. Scaffold poles are stacked on the pavement. A hand-painted sign: REFIT WORKS COMMENCE DAY 0.

INT. CALLOWAY HOUSE, LOBBY - CONTINUOUS

ILSE VARGA (41), municipal lanyard, hard hat in one hand, sets a laser scanner on a tripod in the middle of the lobby. She levels it. She checks the level twice. A cheerful green light sweeps the walls.

GORDON PIKE (67), cardigan, a ring of keys the size of a fist, watches from the door of the caretaker's room.

GORDON
Takes long?

ILSE
A morning per two floors. It will want to be left alone while it thinks.

GORDON
Everything in this building wants that.

She follows the scanner's cable up the central stair, tape and tablet in hand.

INT. CALLOWAY HOUSE, THIRD FLOOR - LATER

Ilse stands at the head of the stair with the tablet. A model of the floor has built itself in grey point-cloud: corridor, eight door frames, the lift shaft.

SECTION: PLANNING REGISTER, THIRD FLOOR - eight flats along a corridor, the rear strip hatched flat grey.

She taps a measure. The volume of the point cloud, then the sum of the drawn rooms. A difference, small, the same on every floor.

ILSE
(to the tablet)
Recalibrate.

She recalibrates. The difference is the same.

## 02. THE SERVICE DOOR

TERMINAL HEADER: DAY 30 / DWELLINGS 48

INT. CALLOWAY HOUSE, SECOND FLOOR REAR - AFTERNOON

A service landing at the back of the building, lit by a single window onto a light well. Pipes along one wall. A narrow door painted the same cream as the plaster, with no handle on the corridor side, a small brass knob on this side.

Ilse follows the scan to where the difference must be hiding. She tries the knob. The door opens.

INT. ROOM 2R - CONTINUOUS

A room of eight square metres. A bed made tight, a kettle on a crate, a radio playing low, a small table. At the table, MAREK (50s), writing a letter with a ballpoint pen.

They look at each other. He puts down the pen.

ILSE
I am so sorry. I thought this was a store.

MAREK
It was. Then it was mine.

ILSE
Do you... live here?

MAREK
Since two thousand and nine. My partner is at work.

He reaches into a drawer without being asked and holds out an electoral card. "RM 2R, CALLOWAY HOUSE."

Ilse checks her tablet. The 1974 plan shows the strip behind the corridor as a hatched blank.

ILSE
(quietly)
It is not on the plan.

MAREK
(politely)
It is on the electoral roll.

## 03. FIVE ROOMS

TERMINAL HEADER: DAY 29 / DWELLINGS 48

INT. CALLOWAY HOUSE, REAR STRIP - DAY

A rapid sequence up the back of the building, each room entered by a narrow service door.

ROOM 3R. OTTILIE BRANDT (84), tiny, upright, in a cardigan buttoned wrong by one button. A single bed, a framed photograph of a man in uniform. She brings out a shoebox before Ilse can speak.

OTTILIE
You will want these.

Ilse opens the box. Letters, bills, bank statements. Most addressed to MR. A. BRANDT.

OTTILIE (CONT'D)
My husband dealt with the post. I only lived here.

ROOM 4R. DOLORES (60s), a radio and a crossword. A library card slid across the table.

ROOM 5R. TEODOR KASK (35), in a courier's jacket, opens a folder labelled with a pen. Six documents, each flagged with a yellow tab: bank, library, electoral roll, mobile account, a parcel firm's address check, a letter from a doctor.

TEODOR
You will need to see that they all say the same. They do.

ROOM 6R. JOSS (20s), earbuds out, hands her a utility bill.

Ilse measures each room with a laser. Her tablet logs the numbers: 7.4, 7.9, 8.1, 8.3, 8.6. All below 9. She pauses, in the last room, at the window. It opens onto the light well. She measures the well. 2.4 metres.

ILSE
(to herself)
Standard says three.

JOSS
Standard says what?

ILSE
Never mind.

CLOSE ON: Ilse's tablet. Five rows. Beside each, a field: DRAWN ON PLAN. The five boxes are empty.
