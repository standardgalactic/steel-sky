# The Department of Unclaimed Rooms: Visual Bible

Catalogue slot 01.01 | Companion to the treatment, scene outline and screenplay | Target form: graphic novel

---

## 1. Visual thesis

The book draws one building seven times. Each register in the story (planning, heritage, electricity, electoral roll, proofs, caretaker's ledger, rehousing plan) is a section of Calloway House drawn from the same viewpoint at the same scale, with its own ink and line weight. Each section includes the rooms that register sees and omits the rest.

- **The measurements never change.** The tape, the scanner and the room dimensions are drawn the same way in every panel. What changes is what a register counts.
- **The orderly page degrades.** The catalogue asks for precise sections and orderly panels that grow harder to reconcile. The grid starts perfectly ruled and goes slightly out of register after each correction, until Act Three separates the sections and rebuilds them as one legend-bearing drawing.
- **No supernatural content.** Nothing in any panel is impossible. The unease is in the paper.

---

## 2. Medium

**Architect's ink linework with flat wash.** Fine technical pen on drafting paper, hairline hatching, flat watercolor wash laid inside the lines. No gradients, no soft shading. The aim is the visual voice of a surveyor's drawing office.

This keeps 01.01 distinct from 03.01 (charcoal, worn print texture, pools of light). It is also deliberately not a looser illustration style, so the register sections stay legible.

---

## 3. Palette

Paper and ink first, then one ink identity per register. The register inks appear only on that register's section and on its documents.
