# The Department of Unclaimed Rooms: Visual Bible

Catalogue slot 01.01 | Companion to the treatment, scene outline and screenplay | Target form: graphic novel

---

## 1. Visual thesis

The book draws one building seven times. Each register in the story (planning, heritage, electricity, electoral roll, proofs, caretaker's ledger, rehousing plan) is a section of Calloway House drawn from the same viewpoint at the same scale, with its own ink and line weight. Each section includes the rooms that register sees and omits the rest.

- **The measurements never change.** The tape, the scanner and the room dimensions are drawn the same way in every panel. What changes is what a register counts.
- **The orderly page degrades.** The catalogue asks for precise sections and orderly panels that grow harder to reconcile. The grid starts perfectly ruled and goes slightly out of register after each correction, until Act Three separates the sections and rebuilds them as one legend-bearing drawing.
- **No supernatural content.** Nothing in any panel is impossible. The unease is in the paper.

---

## 2. Medium

**Architect's ink linework with flat wash.** Fine technical pen on drafting paper, hairline hatching, flat watercolor wash laid inside the lines. No gradients, no soft shading. The aim is the visual voice of a surveyor's drawing office.

This keeps 01.01 distinct from 03.01 (charcoal, worn print texture, pools of light). It is also deliberately not a looser illustration style, so the register sections stay legible.

---

## 3. Palette

Paper and ink first, then one ink identity per register. The register inks appear only on that register's section and on its documents.

| Role | Name | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ground | Drafting Paper | #EFE9DA | Page ground |
| Line | Survey Black | #1C1C1E | Linework, planning register ink, all measurements |
| Cool wash | Slate Wash | #B9C2C9 | Default flat wash for rooms in Acts One and Two |
| Planning register | Survey Black | #1C1C1E | Solid line, standard weight |
| Heritage plans 1931 | Sepia | #7A5C3E | Fine line, slightly faded |
| Electricity | Meter Orange | #D9772B | Dotted line and meter marks |
| Electoral roll | Cobalt | #2F5AA8 | Medium line, numbered |
| Proofs (bank, library, mobile) | Teal | #2E7D7A | Thin line, stamped marks |
| Caretaker's ledger | Oxblood | #8A2B2B | Handwritten line, pencil-ruled |
| Rehousing plan | Slate Violet | #6A5F86 | Heavy line, tabular |
| Warm wash | Lamp Peach | #E8B98A | Domestic warmth, introduced in Scene 10 |
| Brass | Brass | #B58B2A | Keys, door knobs, the final plate |

Rules:

- **Warm wash waits.** Every room is washed in cool Slate Wash through Scene 09. The first warm panel is the flat in 6C (Scene 10). Warmth then spreads: Ottilie's room, the caretaker's room, and the final stair. By Scene 18 the building has colour its sections did not.
- **Brass** appears on every key and knob, but only the plate in Scene 18 is given a highlight.
- **No fifth ink on a register page.** A section uses its own ink plus Survey Black for the building envelope, nothing else.

## 4. The building

**Calloway House.** Six storeys of 1930s brick. Front block with 48 flats, eight per floor from ground-plus-one. Central stair. A narrow rear service strip with a light well 2.4 metres wide between the front block and the rear service stair. Five rear rooms, 2R to 6R, one per floor from the second.

**The reference section (the S view).** A north-facing cross-section through the central stair and the rear strip, six floors tall, front block on the left, rear strip on the right, light well in between. Every section in the book uses this viewpoint and the same scale. The rear strip is the part that changes.

**The cream door.** Each rear room is entered by a service door painted the same cream as the plaster, with no handle on the corridor side and a small brass knob inside. It is drawn flush with the wall. Scenes 02 and 13 must use the same drawing of the same door, so the reader recognizes it as the thing that was always there.

**Interiors.**
- *Rear rooms:* 7.4 to 8.6 m2. A bed, a kettle on a crate, a small table, a single window onto the well. Each is individualized by its occupant's objects but built on the same shell.
- *Flats:* eight per floor, standard layout, with some wear.
- *The three empty flats (2D, 4B, 5F):* drawn with the same care as occupied ones. Stripped mattress, coat hangers, a pile of old mail.

## 5. Cast design

Each character has a silhouette cue, a hand cue and a posture cue.

**Ilse Varga (41).** Hard hat in hand or clipped to her bag, municipal lanyard, tablet on a strap. Practical dark jacket. Tape and laser measure always visible. *Posture:* she looks at a thing, then at the plan, then back. *Visual arc:* her linework in her own drawings is clean and decisive in Act One and slightly crowded in Act Two. In Act Three she draws less and labels more.

**Desmond Okafor (59).** Tie, cardigan under his jacket, reading glasses pushed up on his forehead. *Hand cue:* a finger resting on the page, never a pointing hand. *Posture:* sits on the corner of a desk. He is not drawn as an obstruction; he is drawn at the edge of panels, handling documents.

**Ottilie Brandt (84).** Very small, upright, a cardigan, one button often wrong. A shoebox. *Visual cue:* in every panel she is drawn slightly smaller than her surroundings would suggest, so doors and furniture feel a little large. By Scene 18 her proportion is corrected.

**Teodor Kask (35).** Courier jacket, folder with yellow tabs. *Hand cue:* folding and unfolding paper.

**Gordon Pike (67).** Cardigan, a ring of keys the size of a fist. *Posture:* stands in doorways. Drawn with the key board behind him in most caretaker's room panels.

Secondary: Marek (2R), Dolores (4R), Joss (6R), Nell and Kofi (6C, with a cot and mobile), Pru Haldane (archivist, cotton gloves).
