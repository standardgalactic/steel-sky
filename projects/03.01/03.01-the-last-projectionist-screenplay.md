# THE LAST PROJECTIONIST

Screenplay, catalogue slot 03.01
Draft 1, built on the scene outline

---

## Conventions used on the page

- **COUNT CARD.** Each scene opens on Priya's inventory card: the day number and the number of items still unassigned. It is an observed object, drawn hard-edged.
- **MARKER FRAMES.** Any image of 1987 is introduced by the slug RECONSTRUCTION with its source named. Perforation-edged frames, flickering inner border. The same marker serves every account, true or false.
- **No direct 1987.** Until the closing screening in Scene 18, nothing from 1987 is shown except as someone's drawing, telling, or document.

# ACT ONE: AN EXPLANATION TAKES SHAPE

---

## 01. THE LAST INVENTORY

COUNT CARD: DAY 9 / OPEN 214

INT. MERIDIAN CINEMA, PROJECTION BOOTH - MORNING

A narrow room with two projectors facing two small ports. A rewind bench. Steel shelves of film cans, each labelled in a different decade's handwriting. A paper logbook, spine cracked, on a stand by the door.

AUREL DACEY (63) threads a projector without looking at his hands. His fingers know the path. He checks the loop, closes the gate, lifts one finger to listen to the motor. Everything he does has one right order.

PRIYA ANAND (29) stands in the doorway with a clipboard and a tablet. Municipal lanyard. She has been there several minutes and he has not acknowledged her.

PRIYA
I need to start with the vault. I can work around you.

AUREL
Nobody works around me. They wait.

PRIYA
The survey says everything is screened, transferred, retained or disposed of by the ninth day. That is nine days.

AUREL
Eight and a half. The ninth is a ceremony.

She enters. Takes a can from the shelf, reads the lid, types.

PRIYA
"The Salt Orchard, reel four." Is this the original release print?

AUREL
It is the print.

PRIYA
There is a repair splice at the head. A different stock. So it is a repaired print.

AUREL
It is The Salt Orchard.

PRIYA
It is a version of The Salt Orchard. I need to record which one.

AUREL
(a beat)
You are going to write down every splice.

PRIYA
Every one I can find.

Aurel shuts the projector door a little harder than necessary.

AUREL
Then you will be here longer than the building.

She smiles slightly, not at him. Types.

CLOSE ON: the count card on her tablet. OPEN 214.

## 02. TWO HANDS ON THE LEADER

COUNT CARD: DAY 9 / OPEN 214

INT. PROJECTION BOOTH - AFTERNOON

Aurel at the rewind bench. Reel four of The Salt Orchard runs through his gloved fingers. Priya sits across the bench photographing cans one at a time.

The leader reaches the winders. Pencil on the white tail, faint, in a slanting hand.

INSERT - THE LEADER. Hard-edged, flat. The pencil reads: H.V. INS after cue 7, OUT after, 41 FT. Beneath it, in a different hand, rounder, a tick mark and a second, smaller note: ok - D.

AUREL
Edmund.

PRIYA
The first line?

AUREL
Edmund Hale. Projectionist until ninety-nine. I would know that seven anywhere. He crossed it.

PRIYA
And the second hand?

Aurel looks at the round letters. He does not know them. He moves on.

AUREL
There are more.

He pulls two other cans. The same format on both leaders, different footage counts, the same two hands.

PRIYA
Three prints, same abbreviation.

AUREL
H.V. Harlan Voss. Owner before his son. Edmund was noting an instruction. Insert after cue seven. Out after. Forty-one feet.

PRIYA
(writing)
"H.V." I am recording the initials as written.

AUREL
You can write what it means.

PRIYA
I do not know what it means.

AUREL
I just told you.

PRIYA
You told me what you think it means.

She leaves the field after "H.V." blank. Aurel watches her do it. It annoys him more than a contradiction would have.

## 03. THE SCENE BENEDIKT REMEMBERS

COUNT CARD: DAY 9 / OPEN 209

INT. PELL'S CAFE - EVENING

Warm, low. Six tables. A hatch to the kitchen. A door at the back that once opened into the theatre's overflow lobby. MARGO PELL (74) wipes a counter that is already clean.

At a window table, BENEDIKT LOWE (71), cardigan, a retired schoolteacher's habit of waiting for the whole room to be quiet before speaking. Aurel sits across from him with tea he does not drink.

AUREL
I am working through the old prints. The Salt Orchard.

BENEDIKT
Oh, that one.

AUREL
You remember it?

BENEDIKT
There is a scene I remember. I have told it so often I could do it in my sleep.

AUREL
Go on.

BENEDIKT
A red van. Coming down the lake road. The heroine in the passenger seat. The road bends past the old quarry, the van brakes, she looks back. And there is a cello. One phrase, rising, three notes, then the fourth does not resolve. The whole house went quiet. And then somebody laughed, nervously, and then someone told them to hush.

RECONSTRUCTION (BENEDIKT'S TELLING): marker frames bleed in over the café wall. A road, a van, a cello line drawn as three rising ticks and a fourth that hangs. Unfinished figures in the seats. The frame edges flicker.

Back to the table. Margo has stopped wiping.

AUREL
When was that?

BENEDIKT
The harvest festival. Eighty-seven. The night Daniel went missing. I remember because the whole town was out searching by morning.

Aurel puts his cup down.

AUREL
The scene is not in the print.

BENEDIKT
It should be. I saw it.

AUREL
Someone took it out. After.

Benedikt looks at him, then at the table. The sentence has a weight he is willing to lift.

BENEDIKT
Yes. That would explain it, wouldn't it.

Margo comes over with a plate nobody ordered.

MARGO
Eat. Aurel, the closing programme still wants a second page. Benedikt, you said you would write the welcome.

Her voice is easy. Her eyes are not.

## 04. A MISSING PASSAGE

COUNT CARD: DAY 8 / OPEN 198

INT. MERIDIAN AUDITORIUM - MORNING

Work lights on. 412 seats under dust sheets. A pale rectangle where the proscenium curtain once hung. Priya sits in row H with the tablet on her knees. Aurel stands at the aisle with a light box and a strip of film.

He holds the strip to the light. Priya reads over it.

INT. PROJECTION BOOTH - MORNING

On the rewind bench, reel four unspooled in coils. Aurel sets two lengths side by side, one on each side of the cue-seven mark.

PRIYA
There is nothing here that looks like missing footage. The splices are all from the original build.

AUREL
Count from cue seven to the next frame of action. How much?

PRIYA
The running length is consistent with the schedule. And?

AUREL
And forty-one feet is not a gap you can see. It is a gap you can count.

PRIYA
Then you cannot say it was removed after delivery from the print alone. A print can arrive short.

AUREL
Could a passage have been inserted at the indicated position?

She considers. Real answer.

PRIYA
Technically, yes. At that point in the reel, the cue marks, the leader notes. An insertion at that point is physically possible.

AUREL
Possible.

PRIYA
Possible is not the same as occurred.

He nods, and in his face it has already become confirmation.

## 05. THE FIRST RECONSTRUCTION

COUNT CARD: DAY 8 / OPEN 198

INT. PROJECTION BOOTH - EVENING

Night through the small ports. Aurel at the rewind bench with a roll of butcher paper. A pencil, a ruler, a glass of water he forgets. Behind him the shelves go dim.

He draws. A rectangle for the screen. Beside it a rectangle for the can. A line between them, then a second line, off to one side.

AUREL (V.O., to himself)
Festival night. Edmund runs the insert. The house sees it. Harlan finds out.

RECONSTRUCTION (AUREL'S DRAWING): marker frames form around his pencil strokes. A figure at a desk, indistinct, hands pressing down a sheet of paper. A second figure with scissors. The frames flicker at the edges, then steady.

AUREL (V.O.)
Next morning the scene is cut. Forty-one feet. Gone.

He draws a small van. Stops. Draws a smaller figure inside it.

AUREL (V.O.)
And the same night, Daniel.

The marker frames pull tight. The van gets a face behind the glass. The reconstruction is now vivid, fast, and it contains Harlan Voss at a desk and a boy in a van and a scene cut in a dark office.

Aurel sits back. He is moved by his own drawing.

AUREL
(quietly)
That is the whole thing.

CLOSE ON: the paper. Every line he has drawn is either a leader note, Benedikt's telling, or a line drawn only because it makes the other lines meet. The camera lingers on the longest line, the one joining the van to the cut. Nothing on the bench supports it.

## 06. THE OWNER'S SON

COUNT CARD: DAY 7 / OPEN 171

INT. MERIDIAN AUDITORIUM - MORNING

TOBIAS VOSS (58), good coat, reading glasses on a cord. He walks the aisle with a folder, checking seat numbers against a plan. Aurel meets him halfway.

TOBIAS
The surveyors want the full list of fixtures by Friday. The seats go to the salvage firm, the screen goes with the building.

AUREL
The screen stays for the final showing.

TOBIAS
Of course.

AUREL
We are showing The Salt Orchard. Edmund's leaders have notes. A scene from the festival in eighty-seven. A red van.

Tobias stops. His hand tightens on the folder.

TOBIAS
Where did you hear about a van.

AUREL
Benedikt remembers it.

TOBIAS
That was a long time ago.

AUREL
It was your father's van.

TOBIAS
(low)
We are not going to discuss Daniel.

AUREL
I did not mention Daniel.

A silence. Tobias takes off his glasses.

TOBIAS
Is the closing night going to be used for something? I need to know before it is announced. If it is a platform for accusations, I will cancel.

AUREL
It is a screening.

TOBIAS
I have seen what you do with a record. You build a case and then you find a defendant.

He leaves. His footsteps recede up the aisle.

Aurel stands in the empty house. In his mind the distress is a confession. Priya, at the back of the stalls, writes: Tobias V. declined to discuss D.W. No content given.

CLOSE ON: Priya's note. The word "declined" is hers. The word "refused," which Aurel will use later, is not.

# ACT TWO: COHERENCE BECOMES ACCUSATION

## 07. REHEARSING THE MEMORY

COUNT CARD: DAY 6 / OPEN 143

INT. PELL'S CAFE - AFTERNOON

Aurel spreads three sheets of butcher paper over two tables. Benedikt leans over them with his glasses pushed up. Margo carries a tray past and does not stop.

BENEDIKT
That is the quarry bend. I would know it.

AUREL
And the van enters from the left.

BENEDIKT
From the left. Yes.

RECONSTRUCTION (BENEDIKT'S TELLING, REVISED): marker frames. The van now enters left. A figure stands at the aisle of the auditorium, tall, coat collar up. Frames flicker at the edge.

AUREL
Who was standing at the aisle?

BENEDIKT
(thinking)
Someone tall. By the aisle. Near the back.

AUREL
Tobias Voss.

BENEDIKT
It could have been.

MARGO
(from the counter, not turning)
You sat in the left block, Benedikt. Row C. You could not have seen the back aisle from there. There is a pillar.

BENEDIKT
I turned around.

MARGO
You did not turn around. You never turn around in a theatre.

A pause. Aurel gathers the pages.

AUREL
Details drift. The shape holds.

MARGO
The shape is what you drew, Aurel.

He does not hear it. Or he hears it as a small correction to something already settled.

## 08. THE OVERLOOKED SURFACES

COUNT CARD: DAY 6 / OPEN 143

INT. PROJECTION BOOTH - AFTERNOON

Priya photographs every can on a steel shelf, lid on, lid off, label, rim, tail. Her hands are quick. Aurel stands at the bench with his drawing of the missing passage, trying to size the cut in feet and frames.

PRIYA
This one has a label on the side. Not the lid.

AUREL
(not looking)
Lab labels. They put them on at the lab.

She turns the can under the light. A narrow paper strip, printed.

INSERT - THE LABEL. Wide framing, among the other cans, not isolated. It reads: REGIONAL BOARD DELETIONS 41 FT. A date stamp below, smudged: 14 OCT.

PRIYA
There is a date stamp. October fourteenth.

AUREL
Mm.

PRIYA
Do you have the delivery register for the festival season?

He points at a shelf without turning. She finds the ledger. Pages of columns, a quarter-century of hands.

She lays the ledger beside the can and begins to compare. Aurel walks past her with the paper roll under his arm. He does not look at either.

## 09. THE PUBLIC CLAIM

COUNT CARD: DAY 5 / OPEN 102

INT. PELL'S CAFE - EVENING

The back room. A long table. Aurel stands at the head with his three sheets taped to the wall behind him. Benedikt sits close. Margo at the far end with her arms crossed. Priya takes notes by the door. Tobias has been asked and has come, coat still on.

AUREL
I am going to tell you what happened on the night of the harvest festival in nineteen eighty-seven.

He tells it. The leader notes. The missing scene. The van. Harlan. The cut. Daniel.

RECONSTRUCTION (AUREL'S ACCOUNT): marker frames, fast, a sequence that plays like a film trailer. The desk. The scissors. The van pulling out.

AUREL (CONT'D)
Harlan Voss ordered the scene removed because it showed the family's van on the lake road the night a boy disappeared. That is why the notes say OUT. That is why Benedikt remembers it and the print does not.

TOBIAS
Say what you are claiming about me.

AUREL
Only that you know where the boy went.

TOBIAS
Say which of those things you actually found. In the building. On paper.

AUREL
The notes. The memory. Your silence.

TOBIAS
A silence is not a document.

AUREL
Everything fits.

TOBIAS
Fitting is not the same as being the only thing that fits.

Tobias looks at Margo. Margo looks at the table.

TOBIAS (CONT'D)
(to her, low)
Say something.

She says nothing.

He leaves. The bell over the door rings once.

AUREL
(to the room)
You see.

Priya's pen stops. Benedikt looks from Aurel to Margo and for the first time seems unsure which of them he is afraid for.

## 10. THE COST ARRIVES

COUNT CARD: DAY 4 / OPEN 96

INT. MERIDIAN AUDITORIUM - MORNING

Tobias stands in the aisle, no coat, speaking evenly. Aurel stands three rows down. Priya sits at the end of row F with her tablet closed.

TOBIAS
The closing screening cannot go ahead as you have framed it. Not with that account attached to it.

AUREL
The account is mine. The screening is the town's.

TOBIAS
The town will not be told that my father covered up a death in a film.

AUREL
Nobody said death.

TOBIAS
You said enough. I am not going to touch the reel. I am not going to lock the booth. I am asking you not to turn the last night into a trial.

He goes. The door sighs shut.

Priya opens the tablet.

PRIYA
Which observation establishes that Harlan Voss gave an instruction?

AUREL
The leader. "H.V. INS, OUT."

PRIYA
Read me what is on it.

AUREL
H.V. insert after cue seven, out after, forty-one feet.

PRIYA
(shows the tablet)
This is the transcription. H, period, V, period. Nothing else on it identifies a person. You are reading "Harlan Voss." Nothing on this page says that.

AUREL
Who else is H.V.?

PRIYA
I do not know. That is what I wrote down.

She sets the exact inscription next to his interpretation on the screen. The two lines do not match, and the distance between them is, for a moment, the only thing in the room.

## 11. DELIVERED SHORT

COUNT CARD: DAY 4 / OPEN 88

INT. PROJECTION BOOTH - AFTERNOON

The delivery register and the can with the label lie side by side under the work lamp. Priya has marked the matching rows with a pencil. Aurel reads over her shoulder.

PRIYA
The print came in on the sixteenth of October. The lab label on this can is stamped the fourteenth. The label says forty-one feet had already been deleted by the regional board before the can left the lab.

AUREL
Before delivery.

PRIYA
Before delivery.

AUREL
Then the Meridian never held that scene.

PRIYA
At least not in the delivered print. If the label is what it appears to be.

A long moment. Aurel's face goes through something and arrives at something else.

AUREL
Then it was the board. They cut it at source. Benedikt's memory is real, but it came from somewhere else. The trailer. A press still. Another night.

PRIYA
That fits the label. Does it fit the leader notes?

AUREL
The notes record someone's intention to put it back.

PRIYA
That is a third story.

AUREL
It is the same story. Only the cut is earlier.

RECONSTRUCTION (AUREL'S SECOND ACCOUNT): marker frames, identical in edge and flicker to the first. A board table, a pair of scissors, a lab bench. A trailer unreeling on a wall. The marker does not tell us this is a better account or a worse one. It is exactly as sure of itself as the first.

Priya looks at the frames over his shoulder. She has seen this before, in the same hands.

## 12. A SECOND CERTAINTY

COUNT CARD: DAY 4 / OPEN 88

INT. PELL'S CAFE - EVENING

Benedikt at the window table. Aurel across from him, tea untouched again.

AUREL
The scene could not have been shown on the night. The label proves it.

BENEDIKT
But I saw it.

AUREL
You saw something. A trailer. A photograph in the foyer. You have told it for thirty-eight years.

BENEDIKT
(slowly)
I thought I could see the road bend. Then I thought I could see Tobias at the aisle. Then...

He stops. He is an old man trying to separate two kinds of seeing.

BENEDIKT (CONT'D)
I do not know which parts were mine.

Margo is standing behind Aurel with the pot. She sets it down hard.

MARGO
Last week you told him it happened exactly as he said. This week you tell him it never happened. You are as sure now as you were then.

AUREL
The evidence changed.

MARGO
The evidence did not change your sureness.

AUREL
If you know something, say it.

Margo's mouth opens. She looks at Benedikt, who has aged ten years in a week. She closes it.

MARGO
I gave my word once.

AUREL
To whom?

MARGO
It is not mine to tell.

She leaves for the kitchen. The door swings twice and settles. In the quiet, Benedikt says, to nobody:

BENEDIKT
I think we have been hurting each other.

## 13. TWO CHANGES TOO MANY

COUNT CARD: DAY 2 / OPEN 41

INT. PROJECTION BOOTH - MORNING

The booth is half empty. Cans stacked in transfer crates, labelled in Priya's block capitals. The shelves show their bare steel. The logbook is the last thing left on its stand.

Priya has three documents open: the 1987 festival programme, the reel schedule for the feature, and the logbook, held flat with two clips.

PRIYA
The feature ran on four reels. That is three changeovers. Count the changeovers in the book for the festival night.

AUREL
(reading)
One, two, three. Four. Five.

PRIYA
Five. Two more than the reels need.

AUREL
Edmund was careful. If he logged five, there were five.

PRIYA
The programme lists a nine-minute intermission after reel two. For refreshments. It is scheduled.

She points to the book. Edmund's hand, narrow and tidy.

INSERT - THE LOGBOOK PAGE. Hard-edged, flat. Two lines between the reel-two and reel-three entries: "Lts down again for supp. proj." and below it, "Back to feature."

AUREL
Lights down again.

PRIYA
During the interval. A supplementary projection, and then the return to the feature. That makes the two extra changeovers.

AUREL
(starting)
So the passage was...

He stops. His pencil hovers over the margin where he was about to write.

PRIYA
